No passwords, no OAuth app: passkeys all the way

How people and computers get into a SelfHostArtifacts site, and how you get back in if you lose a device.

From a fresh deploy to your phone

  1. Open the owner link

    The deploy script prints a link that works once. Opening it asks for your name and creates a passkey: you now own the site.

  2. Pair your computer

    artifact setup makes an Ed25519 key on the computer and shows a code; you approve it in the browser. Agents on that computer can now publish.

  3. Add your phone

    Account → Show QR code. Scanning it signs the phone in for two minutes; add a passkey there and the phone signs in on its own from then on.

  4. Invite people

    Settings → Create invite link. Whoever opens it first picks a name and creates a passkey. They can read every artifact, nothing more.

Every way in

Way inWho uses itLifetime
PasskeyEveryone, every dayUntil removed
Invite linkNew viewers (and the first owner)Once, 7 days (owner link: 1 day)

artifact openA computer you pairedOnce, 2 minutes
Lost every passkey?Run artifact open on a paired computer, or pnpm run deploy --owner-link for a new owner link.

What the server stores

Public keys only. Passkeys keep their private keys on your devices, and paired computers keep theirs in ~/.config/artifact. Invite and sign-in links are stored as SHA-256 hashes, so a database leak does not hand out working links.
